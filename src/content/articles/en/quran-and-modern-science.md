The Qur'an introduces itself in these words:

> ذَٰلِكَ ٱلۡكِتَٰبُ لَا رَيۡبَۛ فِيهِۛ هُدٗى لِّلۡمُتَّقِينَ
>
> "This is the Book in which there is no doubt, a guidance for those mindful of Allah."
>
> <cite>— Surah al-Baqarah 2:2</cite>

The Qur'an is first of all a book of guidance, not a textbook of physics or biology. Yet it repeatedly invites people to reflect on the heavens, the earth, water, rain, plants and their own creation.

Discussions of this topic tend to fall into two extremes. Some people link every new scientific discovery to a verse, even when the meaning has to be stretched. Others assume that faith and science must be in conflict. This article takes a balanced path between them: what the verses say, what science says, and the principles to keep in mind when connecting the two.

## The Qur'an's invitation: look, think, reflect

The very first revelation began with reading and knowledge:

> ٱقۡرَأۡ بِٱسۡمِ رَبِّكَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ ۝ خَلَقَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مِنۡ عَلَقٍ ۝ ٱقۡرَأۡ وَرَبُّكَ ٱلۡأَكۡرَمُ ۝ ٱلَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِٱلۡقَلَمِ ۝ عَلَّمَ ٱلۡإِنسَٰنَ مَا لَمۡ يَعۡلَمۡ
>
> "Read in the name of your Lord who created, created man from a clinging substance. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen, taught man what he did not know."
>
> <cite>— Surah al-'Alaq 96:1–5</cite>

And it describes people of understanding this way:

> إِنَّ فِي خَلۡقِ ٱلسَّمَٰوَٰتِ وَٱلۡأَرۡضِ وَٱخۡتِلَٰفِ ٱلَّيۡلِ وَٱلنَّهَارِ لَأٓيَٰتٖ لِّأُوْلِي ٱلۡأَلۡبَٰبِ
>
> "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of night and day are signs for people of understanding."
>
> <cite>— Surah Al 'Imran 3:190</cite>

The next verse praises those who remember Allah standing, sitting and lying down, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth. Remembrance and reflection go together: studying the universe becomes part of worship.

## Five verses that are often discussed

These are the verses most often cited in this discussion. For each, we look at how classical commentators understood it and what is said about it today.

### 1. The stages of human development in the womb

> ثُمَّ خَلَقۡنَا ٱلنُّطۡفَةَ عَلَقَةٗ فَخَلَقۡنَا ٱلۡعَلَقَةَ مُضۡغَةٗ فَخَلَقۡنَا ٱلۡمُضۡغَةَ عِظَٰمٗا فَكَسَوۡنَا ٱلۡعِظَٰمَ لَحۡمٗا ثُمَّ أَنشَأۡنَٰهُ خَلۡقًا ءَاخَرَۚ فَتَبَارَكَ ٱللَّهُ أَحۡسَنُ ٱلۡخَٰلِقِينَ
>
> "Then We made the drop into an *'alaqah*, then We made the *'alaqah* into a *mudghah* (a chewed-like lump), then We made the lump into bones and clothed the bones with flesh; then We brought it forth as another creation. So blessed is Allah, the best of creators."
>
> <cite>— Surah al-Mu'minun 23:14</cite>

The Arabic word *'alaqah* can mean something that clings, a leech, or a clot of blood. Classical commentators generally understood it as a blood clot. Modern embryology describes the early embryo attaching itself to the wall of the womb to draw nourishment, and a few weeks later the raised segments along its back (somites) look like the marks of something chewed. Many Muslim scholars consider this correspondence worth reflecting on. Still, the verse's purpose is to show Allah's power and the wonder of human creation, not to give medical detail.

### 2. "And indeed, We are expanding it"

> وَٱلسَّمَآءَ بَنَيۡنَٰهَا بِأَيۡيْدٖ وَإِنَّا لَمُوسِعُونَ
>
> "And the heaven We built with might, and indeed We are its expander."
>
> <cite>— Surah adh-Dhariyat 51:47</cite>

In the twentieth century, scientists discovered that the universe is expanding. The Belgian astronomer Georges Lemaître proposed the idea in 1927, and observations published by the American astronomer Edwin Hubble in 1929 supported it. Many people today connect the word *musi'un* in this verse with that discovery. Honesty requires adding that classical commentators usually took it to mean "We are vast in power and capacity." Both meanings are possible in Arabic.

### 3. Water and life

> وَجَعَلۡنَا مِنَ ٱلۡمَآءِ كُلَّ شَيۡءٍ حَيٍّۚ أَفَلَا يُؤۡمِنُونَ
>
> "And We made from water every living thing. Will they not then believe?"
>
> <cite>— Surah al-Anbiya 21:30</cite>

Biology tells us that every known living thing on earth needs water to survive, and water makes up most of a living cell. People in every age knew from experience that life needs water, but today we understand how deep that truth goes.

### 4. Everything in its own orbit

> وَهُوَ ٱلَّذِي خَلَقَ ٱلَّيۡلَ وَٱلنَّهَارَ وَٱلشَّمۡسَ وَٱلۡقَمَرَۖ كُلّٞ فِي فَلَكٖ يَسۡبَحُونَ
>
> "And it is He who created the night and the day, and the sun and the moon, each floating in its own orbit."
>
> <cite>— Surah al-Anbiya 21:33</cite>

In the ancient world, a common view was that the sun was fixed in a turning celestial sphere. Modern astronomy tells us the moon orbits the earth, the earth orbits the sun, and the sun itself moves around the centre of our galaxy. The verb *yasbahun*, "they float" or "they swim", is a vivid image of that constant motion.

### 5. The barrier between two seas

> مَرَجَ ٱلۡبَحۡرَيۡنِ يَلۡتَقِيَانِ ۝ بَيۡنَهُمَا بَرۡزَخٞ لَّا يَبۡغِيَانِ
>
> "He released the two seas, meeting one another; between them is a barrier they do not transgress."
>
> <cite>— Surah ar-Rahman 55:19–20</cite>

Where rivers flow into the sea, or two bodies of sea water meet, differences in salt content, temperature and density create a boundary zone. The waters mix only gradually and keep their own properties over a distance. Commentators have always read this as a sign of Allah's power, and ocean science now helps us picture it more clearly.

## Five principles of caution

These verses genuinely invite reflection, but five principles should guide any discussion of them.

**1. The verse is certain; a scientific theory can change.** Science advances and its theories are revised. If we tie a verse to one theory and that theory changes, we put people's faith to a needless test. It is better to say "this verse may point to this discovery" than "the Qur'an has proven this theory".

**2. Respect the Arabic language and the tradition of tafsir.** We should never invent a meaning for a word that Arabic cannot bear. The Andalusian scholar Imam al-Shatibi (d. 790 AH / 1388 CE) criticised, in his book *al-Muwafaqat*, those who searched the Qur'an for every science and art, and insisted that it be understood according to the language and style in which it was revealed.

**3. Avoid false stories.** Social media is full of claims with no evidence, such as the story that the astronaut Neil Armstrong heard the adhan on the moon and became Muslim. Armstrong himself publicly denied this. Spreading such stories does not serve Islam; it damages its credibility.

**4. The Qur'an's true miracle is its guidance.** The truth of the Qur'an does not depend on how much science it contains. Its miracle is its unmatched speech, its guidance, and the transformation it has brought to human lives.

**5. Science is a way of recognising Allah's signs.** The Qur'an itself promises:

> سَنُرِيهِمۡ ءَايَٰتِنَا فِي ٱلۡأٓفَاقِ وَفِيٓ أَنفُسِهِمۡ حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَهُمۡ أَنَّهُ ٱلۡحَقُّۗ
>
> "We will show them Our signs on the horizons and within themselves until it becomes clear to them that it is the truth."
>
> <cite>— Surah Fussilat 41:53</cite>

## The Muslim scientific tradition

This invitation to reflect drew Muslims towards observation and experiment. Ibn al-Haytham (c. 965–c. 1040) wrote the *Kitab al-Manazir* (Book of Optics) on light and vision and insisted on testing ideas by experiment. Al-Biruni (973–c. 1050) measured the radius of the earth and wrote a scholarly study of India. We tell the story of Baghdad's House of Wisdom in a [separate article](/en/articles/house-of-wisdom-golden-age/). These scholars saw science not as a rival to faith but as a way to understand Allah's creation.

## Practical lesson

- This week, read Surah Al 'Imran 3:190–191 with a translation, and recall them when you look at the night sky.
- Before forwarding any "Qur'an and science" post, check the verse number and translation yourself.
- When teaching children science, connect one natural wonder to a verse about it.
