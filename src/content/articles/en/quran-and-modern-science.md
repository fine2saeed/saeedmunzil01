The Qur'an was not revealed as a science textbook; it is a book of guidance. Even so, it repeatedly draws attention to the natural world in ways that invite careful thought.

## An invitation to reflect

Again and again the Qur'an asks the reader to observe the earth, the heavens, the mountains, and the self. The invitation is intellectual, not merely emotional — knowledge is treated as a companion of faith, never its opponent.

> Do they not look at the camels, how they are created?
>
> <cite>— Surah al-Ghashiyah 88:17</cite>

## The path of balance

A word of caution matters here. Forcing every verse to match a current theory is unwise, because scientific theories change over time. The healthier posture is to read the verses reflectively and to pursue knowledge with humility.

This balance is the aim of Saeed Munzil: neither blind imitation nor exaggeration, but understanding the knowledge of faith and world with honesty and adab.
